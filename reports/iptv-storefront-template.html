How 97 IPTV Storefronts Ended Up Sharing One Template

Published 2026-09-17 · Raw counts and method below · Re-runnable

We track 97 IPTV storefronts. On 2026-09-17 we fetched every one of their homepages and compared them against each other rather than against their own marketing. 73 of them publish the same invented feature names, word for word. 67 publish the same price ladder, to the cent. This page is the evidence, with every brand linked so you can check it yourself.

What this report does not claim: we have not established common ownership, and we are not calling any brand here a scam. Shared copy and shared pricing are evidence of a shared supplier and a shared site template — nothing more, and nothing less.

1. The same invented feature names

“Predictive Content Prefetch” is not an industry term. It is not a standard, a protocol or a product. It appears to have been coined for this template, which makes it an unusually clean fingerprint: any two sites using it are drawing on one source. 4. The same site architecture

90 of 97 sites carry a “Channels List” navigation item and 90 carry a Reseller signup page — the latter being the tell that these are storefronts in a distribution chain rather than end operators. 76 publish an Installation Guide section in the same position.

5. Four have already vanished

Since we began tracking, 4 domains have stopped resolving entirely — NXDOMAIN, not downtime: DigitaLizard IPTV, HyperSonic TV, Static IPTV, Yeah IPTV Store. Storefront churn is normal in this market, which is the practical reason not to prepay for a year however good the discount looks.

What this means if you are buying

  1. Ignore the marketing copy. If 73 sites share it, it carries no information about the one in front of you.
  2. Ignore the channel count. The same figure appears across most of the market.
  3. Do not prepay for a year. Four brands in this set are already gone.
  4. Pay by card. A chargeback is the only consumer protection that reliably exists here.
  5. Judge the two things that vary: whether support answers a timed pre-sales question, and whether streams hold up at your peak hour.

Method

Every homepage in our index was fetched once on 2026-09-17 with a standard desktop browser user-agent, following redirects. Scripts, styles and markup were stripped; the remaining text was searched for the marker phrases, published price points, channel and library claims, named player apps, payment processors and support channels. Domain families were derived from the second-level label. Counts here are generated directly from that data and reproduced per-brand on each review page. Sites that blocked us or failed to resolve are counted as no-data rather than silently dropped.

Frequently Asked Questions

Are these IPTV services all the same company?

We cannot prove common ownership from public data, and we do not claim it. What we can show is that 73 of 97 storefronts publish identical invented feature names and 67 publish an identical price ladder to the cent. The most economical explanation is a shared upstream supplier and a shared site template, with separate resellers running the storefronts.

Does this mean these IPTV providers are scams?

No, and we are careful not to say that. A shared template tells you the marketing copy carries no brand-specific information. It says nothing about whether the streams work. It does mean you should ignore the marketing and judge on refund terms, payment method and a peak-hour test.

How did you identify the template?

We fetched the homepage of every provider in our index on 2026-09-17, stripped scripts and markup, and searched the remaining text for distinctive invented phrases, navigation structure, published price points and domain-naming patterns. The per-brand result appears on each provider's own review page.

Which providers are outside the template?

24 of 97 carry none of the five marker phrases: BINGETRAX, DigitaLizard IPTV, Eternal TV, EvesTV, Expedite TV, HyperSonic TV, IPTV Smarters Pro, IPTV Trends. That does not make them better, only less obviously templated.

Why does the shared price ladder matter to a buyer?

Because it removes price as a decision input. When 67 brands charge the same to the cent, a lower headline price is not a bargain and a higher one is not premium. What remains is refund policy, payment method and whether support answers.
