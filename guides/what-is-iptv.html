What Is IPTV? — the Mechanism, and What You Actually Buy

Last updated: 2026-09-17 · Written from our 97-provider dataset

IPTV means television delivered over an internet connection instead of an aerial, a satellite dish or a cable line. That is the whole of the technical definition. Everything else people argue about — legality, quality, whether a given service is trustworthy — is about what is being delivered and by whom, not about the delivery method.

What actually happens when you press play

A broadcaster or aggregator receives a channel feed and encodes it into small chunks, typically a few seconds each. Those chunks sit on servers. Your player asks for them in sequence over ordinary HTTP, the same protocol that serves web pages, and stitches them back into continuous video. Most services use HLS or MPEG-DASH for this; both are standard, both are what Netflix and BBC iPlayer use.

So the mechanism is not exotic or inherently suspect. What differs between a mainstream streaming service and a $14.95-a-month IPTV subscription is not the technology — it is whether the operator holds the rights to the feeds it is chunking up.

What you actually receive when you subscribe

This is the part most explainers skip, and it matters. In our index of 97 IPTV storefronts, almost none deliver their own app with their own login. What arrives in your inbox is a set of credentials — usually an Xtream Codes username, password and portal URL, or an M3U playlist link plus an EPG URL.

You then load those credentials into a third-party player. Across the live sites we track, the most commonly named players are:

The practical consequence: a meaningful share of your viewing experience is determined by the player you choose, not by the subscription. Buffering that looks like a provider fault is sometimes a player with a small buffer setting. Our device setup guide covers this.

Live, on-demand and catch-up

  • Live is a continuous feed you join in progress, exactly like broadcast. This is where delivery problems show up first, because there is no buffer to hide behind at kick-off.
  • Video on demand is a stored library you start and stop at will. Large advertised VOD counts are cheap to inflate — most of our tracked sites claim around 100,000 titles.
  • Catch-up replays a channel's recent output, typically the last few days. It is the feature most often advertised and least often tested by buyers.

What determines whether it looks good

Four things, roughly in order of impact:

  1. Source bitrate. What the operator actually sends. A channel labelled 4K running at 6 Mbps is upscaled 1080p. No site in our index publishes bitrate figures.
  2. Capacity at peak hour. Delivery is shared. A service that is flawless at noon can stutter at 8pm on a Sunday. This is why an off-peak trial proves nothing.
  3. Your connection. Roughly 10 Mbps sustained per HD stream, 25 Mbps for genuine 4K.
  4. Your player and device. Buffer settings and hardware decoding vary enormously between apps and between a current Firestick and a five-year-old Android box.

Only the last two are within your control, and only the second is testable before your refund window closes — by subscribing for one month and watching at your own peak hour.

Is it legal?

The technology is unambiguously legal. The question is always whether the specific service holds licences for the specific channels it carries, and that is not something you can determine from a sales page. Our legality guide sets out how to think about it.

Frequently Asked Questions

What does IPTV stand for?

Internet Protocol Television — television delivered over an internet connection rather than by aerial, satellite or cable. The term describes the delivery method only, not the legality or quality of any particular service.

What do you actually get when you buy an IPTV subscription?

In almost every case, credentials rather than an app: an Xtream Codes username, password and portal URL, or an M3U playlist link. You load those into a third-party player. Across the 93 live storefronts we track, IPTV Smarters and MAG-compatible players are the most commonly named.

How much internet speed does IPTV need?

Roughly 10 Mbps sustained per HD stream and about 25 Mbps for genuine 4K, with headroom on top. That governs your side of the connection only — it does not guarantee the provider sends a high bitrate.

Is IPTV the same as Netflix?

Technically the delivery is similar; both chunk video and serve it over HTTP. The difference is licensing: Netflix holds rights to everything it streams, whereas most IPTV subscription services do not publish any licensing information at all.

Why does IPTV buffer at night but not during the day?

Because delivery capacity is shared between subscribers. Peak-hour congestion is the single most common cause of buffering, which is why a trial run at noon tells you almost nothing.
